# Delivery and scheduler safety

Current runtime rules for scheduled notifications, permission reconciliation and broadcasts. For executed checks and their limits, see [implementation status](IMPLEMENTATION_STATUS.md).

## Delivery rules and exceptions

Ordinary scheduled Zmanim, Daf, Tefillin, Omer and pre-entry time notices require verified calendar coverage and are suppressed throughout the religious Shabbat/Yom Tov interval and the configured operational closure interval. Manual opening and mode=off do not disable religious quiet. Before entry on an ordinary eve, eligible notifications are allowed until their own cutoff. Consecutive Shabbat/festival days remain one protected sequence. `yomtov=true` identifies a festival requiring this protection; a generic holiday category does not. These classifications follow [Hebcal's documented JSON fields](https://www.hebcal.com/home/195/jewish-calendar-rest-api).

A configured **group-closing notice is the explicit exception**: it is sent after a confirmed close, or at the close transition in messages-only mode, while the group is protected. Opening notices require the quiet period to have ended. Private replies, user-requested administrative operations and explicitly scheduled operator broadcasts are separate from routine calendar notification eligibility.

CalendarService evaluates current state after provider I/O. Outbox eligibility stores short-lived evidence bounded by the next religious/operational transition, job expiry, local day, manual override expiry, custom quiet-hour transition and module-specific solar cutoff. Immediately before the native send call, the gateway rechecks current settings, ownership, time, shutdown, local permission debt and delivery reservations after emoji/topic/media preflight. The same check repeats for the basic-emoji fallback and after persistence. No application await occurs between the final fence and transport entry. Clock rollback, changed settings or expired evidence fail closed.

This is not an atomic guarantee about Telegram receipt time: a request already passed to the SDK/network may be accepted later. Real Telegram latency, server flood restrictions, host suspension and clock accuracy still matter. Configure an appropriate early closing buffer for operational needs. Do not interpret a local test as a zero-delay or 100% uptime guarantee.

## Reminder backlog, quiet hours and uncertainty

Persisted due Tefillin reminders that were never dispatched are coalesced to the latest eligible reminder per group. Actual delivery reservations enforce the configured minimum spacing and daily quota in the group's current timezone across settings revisions. Concurrent workers cannot both reserve the same available spacing slot. Uncertain attempted messages keep their frozen payload/random ID and evidence; later logical messages wait conservatively rather than replaying a burst. Multiple uncertain messages retry in a stable order to avoid mutual blocking. Confirmed sends remain confirmed if follow-up bookkeeping fails.

Quiet hours are rechecked at delivery time for ordinary modules, including Omer, not only when planning. A delayed job may defer until quiet hours end if still valid. Overnight hours and both occurrences of repeated DST times are supported; missing wall-clock times are not guessed. Tefillin also checks current weekday, pause date, Chol Hamoed/Tisha B'Av policy, daylight window and sunset safety. Omer checks the actual restoration gap again at the final send fence.

Migration 016 conservatively marks old attempt/delivery evidence. An unknown old attempt is not silently treated as never dispatched. This can intentionally suppress or defer an uncertain reminder; it cannot infer whether Telegram accepted an old request. The permission-provenance rules still apply to unresolved older group snapshots.

## Independent scheduling and failure isolation

Production has separate non-overlapping callbacks: permission reconciliation every 15 seconds, notifications every second, broadcasts every 15 seconds. A callback's work time adds to its interval; these are not precise delivery deadlines. Permission reconciliation uses four rolling workers across the keyset traversal: one slow peer occupies one slot while free workers continue to later groups, including across page boundaries. Every launched task is joined before completion. Native peer resolution/reconnection still has SDK-controlled latency; four simultaneous slow peers can occupy all slots, and the health probe reports an overdue completed pass. A detached timeout wrapper is not used to pretend those reads stopped. It queues lightweight close/open jobs without fetching message templates/providers on that path. Already completed restoration/prelocked no-ops avoid unnecessary Telegram permission reads.

Notification planning uses stable keyset traversal of at most 20 active groups per cycle, preserving its cursor for fairness. Disabled modules skip provider planning. Planning and delivery have elapsed-time budgets between operations; a single in-flight request can exceed a budget. Each cycle processes at most 20 outbox jobs and 20 cleanup jobs. Retries persist with backoff. These limits are local work bounds, not a claim that Telegram permits that many sends per group.

An empty/future-only broadcast queue does not start extra permission sweeps. Scheduled broadcasts no longer occupy the permission callback. Manual and scheduled broadcast managers share one cancellation-aware admission queue, so only one native bulk operation runs at a time; authorization is checked again after waiting. Completed in-memory native progress history is capped at the latest 128 fully drained entries while active work and persistent history are preserved. BroadcastManager's optional runtime hooks check current creator authorization, use cancellation-aware waits, cancel remaining queues on shutdown and join launched worker/progress futures. Native `FLOOD_WAIT` is returned to the manager instead of allowing a two-day hidden SDK sleep. The patch is applied offline with exact-file hashes and includes the upstream baseline, prior reviewed source and unified diffs.

Telegram documents per-chat, per-group and bulk message limits; actual MTProto restrictions remain server-controlled. Respect [Telegram's bot limits](https://core.telegram.org/bots/faq#my-bot-is-hitting-limits-how-do-i-avoid-this) and [MadelineProto's flood-wait guidance](https://docs.madelineproto.xyz/docs/FLOOD_WAIT.html). No paid broadcasts are enabled by this update.

Provider requests share a single in-flight result per key and have a short failed-key cooldown. Three host failures trigger a 30-second circuit cooldown below the persistent cache, so previously cached data remains available for coverage validation. Deliberate caller cancellation does not poison shared requests. Provider failure never becomes an invented empty holiday calendar.

## Monitoring and routine history retention

`php bin/health.php` reports separate permission/notification heartbeat ages, completed cycle group counts/durations, due/active/uncertain jobs, oldest due age, recent sends/expiry and maximum recent delivery delay. Notification activity cannot make a stuck permission lane appear fresh. Heartbeat replacement is atomic. Normal polling checks the connection/schema and current metrics without a full database integrity scan; use `php bin/health.php --deep` for explicit full integrity/foreign-key validation during maintenance. A missing, future-dated or older-than-180-second heartbeat marks health unhealthy; operators should investigate before restarting repeatedly.

At most once per minute, bounded maintenance selects up to 200 eligible terminal jobs and removes at most 200 associated delivery-log rows per pass. A job is deleted only after all its logs have been removed. Other supported categories also remove at most 200 eligible rows older than 90 days per pass. It prunes routine terminal jobs/logs, old cache/conversation state and safe routine audit/deletion history. It preserves active work, uncertain dispatch evidence, permission snapshots/operations, unresolved recovery, current last-message pointers, unprocessed bookkeeping and interactive Tefillin acknowledgement rounds/votes. This is **not a globally bounded database-size promise**: retained recovery/interaction history can grow and requires monitoring, backups and an explicit archival policy. No VACUUM or unbounded bulk deletion runs in the scheduler.

## Verification and deployment limits

Regression tests cover slow provider/topic/emoji/media work crossing entry, direct and fallback sends, actual quiet hours, Omer restoration changes, persisted backlog, daily caps/spacing, uncertainty across restart, migration, fair traversal, bounded concurrency and shutdown. Queue tests simulate 1,000 groups with 3,000 due reminders and 180 virtual days with 7,200 deliveries. Application and broadcast load tests use isolated databases, controlled clocks and fake transports; they do not send messages or measure real Telegram capacity.

These simulations do not establish real Telegram capacity, holy-day acceptance or a wall-clock soak. Keep target-host acceptance separate from offline regression results.
